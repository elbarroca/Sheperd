# 16-Week Engagement Plan

## Document status

**Internal proposal — not an executed agreement.** This is a cleaned public copy of the complete supplied message. It records Avi's proposed framework for discussion and immediate execution; it does not prove that the engagement, authority, targets, claims, or activation plan were approved.

## Original message

Hi Michael,

Thank you for taking the time and share your vision and plan.

Your strategic thinking is exactly what we need.

I want to share how I envision your first 16 weeks with SheperD — not as a final plan, but as a framework for discussion and immediate execution.

I'm attaching the engagement principles. Once we align, we start working together right away.

### My vision: how you step in, learn, build, and lead

This is a journey across three distinct phases. Each phase builds on the last. Your role evolves from learner → builder → leader.

### Phase 1: Deep immersion and foundation building (Weeks 1–2)

You step in and immerse yourself in the domain, understand our value proposition, learn about the customer journey, and start building the infrastructure you'll need.

#### What “stepping in” means

- Work closely with me (Avi) and the team to understand how we operate.
- Learn the maritime/D&D regulatory backdrop deeply enough to speak credibly to importers.
- Map the customer decision-maker landscape: who has pain, who can approve, and who provides data.
- Analyze Tier 1 and Tier 2 importer lists I provide; segment by geography, size, and industry pain.
- Identify our strongest partner ecosystem: brokers, 3PLs, and consultants who already have importer trust.
- Understand which business model fits which customer profile; this will emerge from customer conversations.

#### What you build

- Talk tracks tailored to three stakeholder personas: Finance, Supply Chain, and Operations.
- Objection library: every objection I've heard, with credible responses.
- Sales playbook components: discovery questions, data-request checklist, and client handoff checklist.
- CRM architecture that will serve us for the next two years.
- Partner roster with outreach strategy and referral SLA.
- Weekly work rhythms and governance: standup cadence, sync structure, and reporting.
- Strategy for [sheperd.io](https://sheperd.io/): audit the current state, identify gaps, and plan lead-generation enhancements.
- LinkedIn and digital presence roadmap: content pillars, posting cadence, and podcast/speaking opportunities.

#### By the end of Week 2

You are no longer just a generalist business development lead. You speak SheperD fluently, understand why importers need us, know our customer segments and geographic priorities, have a plan to amplify our digital footprint, and have built the foundational tools you'll use for the next 14 weeks.

### Phase 2: Gradual market activation and learning by doing (Weeks 3–12)

You begin reaching out, but in a controlled, disciplined way. You're simultaneously executing and learning: every conversation refines your understanding, and every refinement improves the next conversation.

#### What “gradual responsibility” means

- Weeks 3–6: Run a “warm intro sprint” focused on the highest-potential prospects from my list and partner introductions. Make 50+ outreach attempts, aiming for 15–20 meetings and 3–5 data submissions.
- Weeks 7–12: Run three controlled account sprint cycles with refined segmentation and messaging. Each sprint teaches you which segments respond, which objections matter, and which entry points work.
- Track everything in CRM. Weekly reporting shows me who responded, who stalled, and where the bottlenecks are.
- Amplify digital presence: publish case studies, post on LinkedIn, identify podcast/speaking opportunities, and refine [sheperd.io](https://sheperd.io/) based on early customer feedback.
- Map business models. Finance, Supply Chain, and Operations each have different motivations, buying cycles, and success metrics. Document the patterns.

#### What you're learning

- Which importer segments—industry, size, and region—are most receptive.
- Which stakeholder titles open doors. Finance, Supply Chain, and Operations have different motivations.
- What messaging moves people from “interested” to “submitting data.”
- Which partners are high-quality referral sources versus tire-kickers.
- Where deals stall and why: scope, timing, or internal approval.
- Which business model resonates with which customer archetype.

#### By the end of Week 12

You've closed 3–5 deals. You have a validated playbook: proven messaging, proven segments, and proven entry points. You can tell me exactly what to scale and where the process is bulletproof. You've built a digital presence that generates inbound leads. You understand our customer archetypes.

### Phase 3: Ownership and scale readiness (Weeks 13–16)

You own the domain. You're no longer learning about the customer; you are the expert. The playbook is documented. The team knows how to execute. You're ready to build a team or scale the operation.

#### What “full ownership” means

- Document everything: messaging, discovery approach, objection handling, deal-flow management, and business-model mapping.
- Optimize the funnel. Find the bottleneck—outreach volume, meeting conversion, or data submission—and remove obstacles systematically.
- Digital amplification: [sheperd.io](https://sheperd.io/) is a lead-generation machine, LinkedIn generates inbound interest, and you've identified two or three speaking/podcast opportunities.
- Prepare for scale. If we close funding, define the first hire and how to onboard them on your playbook.
- Customer archetypes: define profiles such as Importer A (Finance-driven) and Importer B (Operations-driven), and know which approach works for each profile.

#### By the end of Week 16

You've proven the commercial model. The playbook is scalable. The team is confident in you. You're ready to either lead a larger team or hand off to a strong second-in-command. You are no longer ramping; you're operating.

### The framework: seven axes, all advancing in parallel

You won't become an expert in all seven axes at once. But each week, progress moves forward on all seven. They're interdependent:

1. Domain Knowledge: maritime, D&D, OSRA, and importer operations.
2. Value Proposition and Customer Journey: messaging, stakeholder motivation, and business-model mapping.
3. Technology and CRM: data architecture, pipeline tracking, and reporting discipline.
4. Sales Infrastructure and Marketing Assets: playbook, checklists, templates, and case studies.
5. Digital Presence and Content: the [sheperd.io](https://sheperd.io/) website, LinkedIn strategy, and podcast/speaking opportunities.
6. Customer and Partner Segmentation: Tier 1 and Tier 2 importer analysis, partner roster, and geographic mapping.
7. Work Rhythms and Governance: daily/weekly cadence, accountability, and reporting.
8. Live Sales Execution: customer conversations, deals, pipeline, and business-model learning.

These aren't silos. Your domain knowledge (Axis 1) makes your messaging (Axis 2) credible. Your digital presence (Axis 5) generates inbound interest. Your customer conversations (Axis 8) teach you which business model fits which profile. Everything compounds.

### Engagement principles

- **You're not alone:** I will work with you weekly through strategy syncs, deal reviews, and obstacle removal. This is not a sink-or-swim role. You have support.
- **Learning is work:** Weeks 1–2 are heavy on learning that is your job. You're not expected to run meetings and close deals in Week 1. You're building the foundation.
- **We start now:** Once we align on principles, we begin work together immediately. The formal legal agreement will follow; it won't hold us up. Speed matters.
- **Transparency and feedback:** We'll discuss what's working and what isn't. I'm open to your ideas on how to adjust. This framework is a starting point, not a decree.
- **Measurement matters:** We'll track outreach volume, meeting conversion, deal progression, bottlenecks, and customer-archetype patterns. Numbers keep us honest.
- **Growth trajectory:** Your responsibility grows each phase. Week 1: you learn. Week 6: you're executing. Week 16: you're owning the domain and training others.
- **Aggressive timeline:** Sixteen weeks is the plan. We're aiming to complete it faster if possible. Speed is an advantage; we learn quickly and adapt faster than competitors.

### Next steps

2. **Confirm agreement and start immediately upon alignment.** Once you're comfortable with the principles, we start. Tomorrow, next Monday, whenever works. No delays. We begin Phase 1 immersion while counsel prepares the formal legal agreement in parallel.
3. **Formal legal agreement in parallel.** Counsel will prepare a binding engagement agreement based on the engagement principles. We'll review and sign it, but this won't hold up our start date. We work first, formalize second.

### What we don't have—and what you'll build

- We don't have a warm list to hand you, but we have the data to create a prioritized outreach list.
- We don't have a polished [sheperd.io](https://sheperd.io/) website or LinkedIn presence; you will improve it as we go.
- We don't have documented business-model mapping; you'll discover this through customer conversations.

Read through this. Share your thoughts. Questions? Let's talk.
