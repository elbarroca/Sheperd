# D&D and OSRA Primer

> [!warning] Boundary
> This is an operational primer, not legal advice. Apply the current rule, contract, tariff, invoice date, facts, and chosen complaint route with a qualified reviewer.

## Definitions

- **Demurrage:** charges for cargo remaining in marine-terminal space beyond allowed free time.
- **Detention:** charges for continued use of carrier equipment outside the terminal beyond allowed free time.
- **BCO:** beneficial cargo owner; often the importer or exporter whose goods move under the bill of lading.
- **VOCC:** vessel-operating common carrier.
- **NVOCC:** non-vessel-operating common carrier.
- **MTO:** marine terminal operator.

## Legal timeline

- **2020-05-18:** FMC interpretive rule on reasonable D&D practices became central to the incentive principle.
- **2022-06-16:** OSRA 2022 became law; relevant self-executing billing requirements took effect without a phase-in.
- **2024-05-28:** 46 CFR Part 541 final billing rule took full effect, including invoice-content requirements after OMB clearance.
- **2025-09-23:** The D.C. Circuit set aside §541.4, the provision that limited who could receive an invoice. The rest of Part 541 remained effective; current eCFR marks §541.4 as reserved.
- **2026-07-08:** The FMC reported that the D.C. Circuit upheld its fact-specific finding that detention charged during a three-day port closure was unreasonable because the charge could not incentivize an earlier return.

## Current Part 541 operating rules

- Do **not** use the original 2024 categorical rule limiting invoices to a contracting party or consignee; §541.4 was vacated in 2025. Current §541.6 still requires the invoice to state why the billed party is the proper party of interest and liable for the charge.
- VOCCs and MTOs generally must issue invoices within 30 calendar days after charges last accrue.
- An NVOCC generally has 30 calendar days from the **issuance date** of the D&D invoice it received to issue its invoice.
- When an NVOCC is both billed and billing party for the same charge, notice that its billed party disputed the charge requires the NVOCC's billing party to provide an additional 30 calendar days for the NVOCC to dispute it.
- A billing party that invoices the wrong person may issue a corrected-party invoice only within 30 calendar days from the date the charge was last incurred.
- Required invoice information covers identifying, timing, rate, dispute, and certification information.
- Failure to include required information eliminates the billed party’s obligation to pay the applicable charge; this does not automatically prove a permanent refund because a compliant invoice may be reissued within applicable timing rules.
- A billed party must receive at least 30 calendar days to request fee mitigation, refund, or waiver.
- After a timely request, the billing party must attempt to resolve it within 30 calendar days unless both parties agree to more time.

## The three-year distinction

46 U.S.C. §41301 says a complainant may seek reparations if a complaint is filed within three years after the claim accrues. This is a limitations rule for a complaint—not a universal promise that every invoice from the prior 36 months is refundable.

Case review must distinguish:

1. Carrier or terminal dispute deadline.
2. Part 541 invoice and dispute timing.
3. FMC charge-complaint process.
4. Formal or small-claims limitation and remedy.
5. OSRA applicability date and non-retroactivity questions.
6. Contract, tariff, evidence, causation, and payment facts.

## Evidence commonly needed

- Invoice and bill of lading.
- Container and shipment identifiers.
- Free-time rule, tariff, service-contract terms, and rate.
- Availability, pickup, return, gate, and terminal timestamps.
- Appointment availability and denial evidence.
- Holds, closures, disruptions, emails, and screenshots.
- Proof of payment or credit.
- Carrier dispute history and responses.

## Recovery routes

- Direct request to billing party for mitigation, refund, or waiver.
- FMC Charge Complaint under 46 U.S.C. §41310 for common-carrier charges. MTO-only charges are excluded unless assessed on behalf of a carrier.
- FMC formal or small-claims complaint where appropriate.
- FMC dispute-resolution assistance.
- Counsel-led or contract-specific process.

The FMC states that investigations do not represent the complainant and do not guarantee refunds.

Charge Complaints are separate from formal and small-claims complaints and cannot be combined with them in one filing.
