# Market and Competitive Landscape

## Evidence-based market signal

The FMC reports that nine major carriers collected roughly **$15.4B** in D&D charges from 2020-04-01 through 2025-03-31. The 2024 final rule also cites approximately **$8.9B billed** and **$6.9B collected** over a two-year 2020–2022 period. These figures establish material spend—not the unlawful, disputable, or recoverable share.

No defensible public evidence found in this research validates SheperD’s $2.1B annual unlawful-charge or $6.2B recoverable estimates.

The FMC’s FY2025 report says about **$6.1M** had been voluntarily waived or refunded through Charge Complaints since OSRA, including $2.89M in FY2025. That shows enforcement value and also how small the visible complaint recoveries are relative to total charges; it is not a recovery-rate or TAM estimate. See [[10_Sources/Source - FMC FY2025 Charge Complaints]].

## Current regulatory/market shifts

- §541.4’s categorical billing-party restriction was vacated in 2025; public competitor material may still repeat the old rule.
- An April 28, 2026 appellate opinion upheld the FMC’s fact-specific finding that detention during a three-day port closure was unreasonable when earlier equipment return was not practically possible; the FMC reported the decision on July 8, 2026.
- The commercial wedge is therefore evidence assembly and deadline discipline—not a simplistic “all operational obstacles invalidate fees” rule.
- Objective recovery and error-rate advertising should be supported before publication under [[10_Sources/Source - FTC Advertising Substantiation]].

## Beachhead hypothesis

Prioritize U.S.-importing BCOs with:

- Meaningful recurring container volume and D&D spend.
- Centralized finance ownership of recovery.
- Accessible invoices, tariffs/contracts, and operational evidence.
- Concentration across a manageable set of carriers and ports.
- A visible owner for supply-chain operations and data.
- Sufficient claim value to support contingency economics.

This is a hypothesis until CRM cohorts show response, validated data, recovery potential, cycle time, and economics.

## Beachhead signal hierarchy

Use these signals to admit accounts for research; never treat them as automatic qualification or expected recovery.

| Signal | Why it may matter | Evidence required | Current confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurring D&D invoices or finance-coded accessorials | Creates a reviewable problem and economic owner | Permitted aggregate count/period and finance owner | Low until CRM data |
| Concentrated port/carrier exposure | Narrows tariffs, portals, evidence patterns, and reviewer load | Carrier/port mix and period; no raw shipment data in vault | Low |
| Centralized finance/AP ownership | May improve recovery reconciliation and fee approval | Named role, current process, and decision path | Low |
| Named logistics/operations owner | Enables event chronology and missing-evidence resolution | Named role and evidence-system map | Low |
| Accessible invoices plus operational evidence | Reduces submission and validation friction | Metadata-only readiness checklist | Medium as a workflow requirement |
| Active invoice, audit backlog, deadline, carrier/portal change, or margin review | May create timing urgency | Dated trigger and source | Low until observed |
| Sufficient product/reviewer capacity | Prevents GTM from exceeding delivery ability | Current throughput, backlog, exception, and SLA evidence | Unknown |

Company size, annual revenue, and total TEU volume alone are weak proxies. Port authority totals such as SRC-040 establish concentrated trade activity, not which accounts incur disputable D&D.

## Buyer and buying-group decision map

| Stakeholder | Incentive | Trigger | Objection or risk | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CFO / VP Finance / Controller | Realized, auditable net value and predictable accounting | Material charge review, margin pressure, audit finding | Speculative recovery, ambiguous credit, hidden workload | Recovery definition, reconciliation method, fee terms, permission-controlled proof |
| AP / freight audit | Accurate invoices, fewer exceptions, clean close | Duplicate/incomplete invoice, backlog, recurring adjustment | Another queue or manual upload burden | Required fields, exception workflow, secure intake, status SLA |
| Supply chain / logistics | Fewer avoidable charges and escalations | Port/carrier disruption, repeated lane issue, closure/hold | Carrier-relationship damage or operational blame | Factual timeline, case boundary, collaboration model |
| Operations / data owner | Bounded request and minimal rework | Approved audit or dispute request | Fragmented systems and unclear ownership | Exact metadata checklist, approved system, missing-item process |
| Legal / procurement | Controlled authority, liability, terms, substantiation | Vendor onboarding, dispute authority, public claim | Legal overreach, unclear contract, customer-data exposure | Entity, authority, MSA/SOW, reviewer boundary, insurance as required |
| Security / privacy | Minimal, governed processing | Data-transfer request or enterprise review | D3 exposure, subprocessors, retention, transfers | DPA, system map, access/logs, retention/deletion, incident process |

These are role hypotheses grounded in workflow requirements, not interviewed-buyer evidence.

## Alternatives and competitors

| Category | Examples | Buyer reason | SheperD implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal AP/logistics process | Spreadsheets, carrier portals, staff | Existing control and no outside fee | Win on effort saved, evidence quality, and realized recovery. |
| Carrier/terminal dispute directly | Billing-party process | Fastest first route | SheperD must show process discipline and follow-through. |
| FMC routes and counsel | Charge complaint, ADR, formal/small claims | Enforcement or higher-stakes remedy | Do not imply SheperD replaces legal advice or FMC representation. |
| Freight audit/pay providers | Broad invoice-audit vendors | Existing data and workflow | Partner or compete on D&D depth and recovery execution. |
| Niche D&D recovery/automation | BlueCargo, Unwaived, HarborClaim, AuditDray, MiraLedger | Focused audit, evidence, dispute, tracking | Public sites show close positioning; operating maturity and outcomes require diligence. |
| Prevention/visibility tools | Container tracking and exception tools | Avoid charges before they occur | Potential upstream partner or product adjacency. |
| Broad drayage invoice audit | Dockline and adjacent audit tools | Validate contracted accessorials before payment and produce evidence | Distinguish D&D specialization from broader invoice control only after product proof. |

## Differentiation to prove

- Better recovery rate on comparable, eligible claims.
- Faster evidence-to-dispute and dispute-to-credit cycle.
- Lower customer workload without weaker control.
- Transparent audit trail accepted by finance and operations.
- Secure, low-friction intake.
- Carrier/port-specific knowledge that improves outcomes.
- Economics that remain attractive after the success fee.

## Do not claim yet

- TAM based on total D&D charges as if all were invalid.
- Market leadership, superior recovery rate, or unique automation.
- Six/seven-figure typical recoveries.
- A validated segment before comparable cohort evidence.
